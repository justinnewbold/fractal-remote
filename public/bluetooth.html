Bluetooth (beta) setup

Fractal Remote · last updated 10 October 2026

Bluetooth is new (beta), and it may not work correctly with every unit or adapter. It lets the phone control your unit with no computer, through a Bluetooth MIDI adapter plugged into the unit.

So far it has been tried on one AM4, from an iPhone, with the three parts below. It has not been tried yet on an FM3, FM9 or Axe-Fx III, or from an Android phone.

The usual way, with your unit on a USB cable to a computer running the desktop app, is not affected and keeps working as it always has.

On this page: what it is · what works · what you need · hooking it up · settings on the unit · connecting in the app · when it doesn’t work · other adapters

What it is

A Fractal unit has no Bluetooth of its own, and it can’t be the one in charge of a USB connection either. So a small box does that job for it: the CME WIDI Uhost. It talks Bluetooth to your phone, and it runs a CME C2MIDI Pro cable whose two plugs go into the unit’s MIDI In and MIDI Out. That is why the Uhost needs power of its own, from a phone charger or a power bank.

In the app it is under Settings → Phone & computer → Bluetooth (beta). It is part of the full version, so the Bluetooth (beta) card shows once the app is unlocked. If you don’t see it, update the app from the App Store or Google Play. You can pick an AM4, FM3, FM9 or Axe-Fx III, but so far only the AM4 has been tried. The VP4 isn’t offered over Bluetooth.

What works over Bluetooth, and what doesn’t

Presets, scenes, names, effects on and off, channels, tempo and the tuner. While Bluetooth is on, the phone doesn’t use the computer at all, and Stop using Bluetooth hands it back to the computer.

What’s different from USB, the same list the app shows:

  • No editing or saving over Bluetooth.
  • Slower to load the preset list.
  • Footswitch changes may take a moment to show.
  • About 30 feet of range.
  • The adapter connects to one thing at a time.
  • On the AM4, scene names don’t show over Bluetooth.
  • No looper, volume button, unit metronome click or Footswitches page.

On the AM4, scene names the phone has already seen through the computer still show. On an FM9 or Axe-Fx III, the extra blocks (Amp 2, Cab 2, Drive 3 and so on) don’t show as pedals over Bluetooth yet.

Before the first time: update the Uhost

CME asks for this before first use. Install CME’s free WIDI app on your phone, power up the Uhost on its own (with no other WIDI switched on nearby), connect to it in the WIDI app, and update both its USB firmware and its Bluetooth firmware. Each takes about two minutes. Then close the WIDI app: the Uhost connects to one thing at a time, and the WIDI app would keep hold of it.

Hooking it up

The C2MIDI Pro’s two MIDI plugs are marked. The black one says [TO MIDI IN] and goes to the unit’s MIDI IN. The white one says [TO MIDI OUT] and goes to the unit’s MIDI OUT. The Uhost has two USB-C sockets: the left one is marked USB Host/Device, the right one USB Power.

AM4

  1. Turn the AM4 on and let it finish starting up. Leave the Uhost without power for now.
  2. Push one LOOTOOLS adapter’s small plug all the way into the AM4’s MIDI IN jack, and the other into its MIDI OUT jack. The two adapters are the same, so either can go in either jack.
  3. Plug the C2MIDI Pro’s black plug [TO MIDI IN] into the adapter in MIDI IN, and its white plug [TO MIDI OUT] into the adapter in MIDI OUT.
  4. Plug the C2MIDI Pro’s USB-C plug into the Uhost’s left socket, USB Host/Device.
  5. Last, power: a USB-C cable from a phone charger or power bank into the Uhost’s right socket, USB Power. 5 volts only. The Uhost has no power switch: it starts when the power goes in, and it powers the C2MIDI Pro too.
  6. Check the lights. The Uhost’s right light goes steady green: it has found the cable and has power. Its left light flashes blue slowly: it is waiting for the phone. The C2MIDI Pro’s green light is on.

FM3, FM9 and Axe-Fx III

  1. Turn the unit on and let it finish starting up before the Uhost gets power. People have reported a unit freezing while it starts up when a MIDI adapter is already live on it.
  2. Plug the C2MIDI Pro’s black plug [TO MIDI IN] into the unit’s MIDI IN.
  3. Plug the white plug [TO MIDI OUT] into the unit’s MIDI out. On an FM3 or FM9 that is the jack marked MIDI OUT/THRU. On an Axe-Fx III it is MIDI OUT, not the separate THRU jack beside it: THRU only repeats what goes in, so the phone would hear itself and never the unit.
  4. Plug the C2MIDI Pro’s USB-C plug into the Uhost’s left socket, USB Host/Device.
  5. Last, power: a phone charger or power bank into the Uhost’s right socket, USB Power. 5 volts only.
  6. Check the lights, as for the AM4: steady green on the Uhost’s right, slow blue flashing on its left.

About power banks. All of this draws very little power, and some power banks switch themselves off when that little is drawn, which cuts the Uhost off after a minute or so. A wall charger never does. If you use a power bank, use one with a low-current mode, or one that doesn’t switch itself off.

Settings on the unit

  • AM4, FM3 and FM9: Setup → MIDI/Remote → MIDI Thru: Off. With it on, the unit sends the phone’s own messages straight back, and the app says “MIDI Thru is on”. Off is the usual setting.
  • Axe-Fx III: there is no MIDI Thru setting. Just keep the white plug in OUT, not THRU.
  • Nothing else needs changing on the AM4.
  • FM3, FM9 and Axe-Fx III, if presets don’t change from the phone: in Setup → MIDI/Remote, set Program Change on, the MIDI channel to 1 or Omni, and PC Mapping off.
  • Leave the C2MIDI Pro’s button alone. A short press switches it to another of its own presets, and holding it for more than 5 seconds resets it.

Connecting in the app

Open Settings (the gear at the top right) → Phone & computer → Bluetooth (beta). The page has two steps, with What you need between them.

1 · Which unit is the adapter plugged into?

Tap AM4, FM3, FM9 or Axe-Fx III. To change it later, tap Stop first.

What you need

The parts for the unit you picked, each with a View on Amazon button, then how they plug in: the same as on this page. Once an adapter has been used on the phone, this folds away behind Show the parts and how they plug in. Full setup guide opens this page.

2 · Connect

On an iPhone

  1. Tap Connect. The first time, the iPhone asks whether Fractal Remote may use Bluetooth: tap Allow.
  2. Apple’s Bluetooth screen opens. Tap your adapter (the Uhost shows up as WIDI Uhost Bluetooth), wait until it says Connected, then tap Done.
  3. The phone does the rest. It says “Connecting to …”, then “Connected · asking the AM4…” (or your unit), then “Connected · AM4 answering” in blue. That is it working.

Connect it in the app, not in the iPhone’s Settings → Bluetooth. A Bluetooth MIDI adapter doesn’t connect from there; Apple’s screen inside the app is the way.

On Android

  1. Tap Connect. The first time, Android asks you to allow Nearby devices for Fractal Remote: allow it. On Android 11 and older it asks for Location instead, because Android needs that to look for any Bluetooth device: allow it, and make sure Location is switched on. The app uses it only to find the adapter, never to see where you are (privacy page).
  2. The phone looks for the adapter for ten seconds, then connects to it. If more than one is nearby, it lists them: tap yours.
  3. If Android shows a Bluetooth pairing request, tap it and accept.
  4. It says “Connected · AM4 answering” (or your unit) in blue when it is working.

Next time, and going back to the computer

  • The button says Connect to WIDI Uhost Bluetooth: it uses the adapter from last time. Use a different adapter is under it.
  • Stop using Bluetooth goes back to the computer.

When it doesn’t work

Check the lights first

  • Uhost, right light: steady green means it has found the C2MIDI Pro and has power. Off means no power in the USB Power socket, or the C2MIDI Pro is in the wrong socket: it goes in the left one.
  • Uhost, left light: a slow blue flash means it is waiting for the phone. Steady blue means it is connected. A fast blue flicker means messages are going through.
  • Left light light blue (turquoise): it has connected itself to another Bluetooth MIDI gadget nearby, such as another WIDI. Switch the other one off.
  • Left light green: it is in the middle of a firmware update. Finish the update in CME’s WIDI app.
  • The C2MIDI Pro’s green light should be on, and flickers when messages go through.

“Adapter connected, but the AM4 isn’t answering”

  • Check the plugs, and swap them if they are the wrong way round. The black plug [TO MIDI IN] goes to the unit’s MIDI IN, the white plug [TO MIDI OUT] to its MIDI OUT. The wrong way round, nothing gets through either way.
  • On an AM4, check both adapters are pushed all the way into the jacks, and that they are Type A.
  • On an Axe-Fx III, the white plug goes in MIDI OUT, never THRU.
  • Check MIDI Thru is off on the unit (above).

“An AM4 answered, not the FM3” (or another pair)

The wrong unit is picked in step 1. Tap Stop, pick the right unit, then tap Connect.

The phone can’t find the adapter, or can’t connect to it

  • Only one phone or computer at a time. If something else has the adapter, the phone can’t have it: CME’s WIDI app left open, another phone or iPad, or a Mac. A Mac that has connected to it before grabs it again by itself whenever it is nearby: disconnect it on the Mac (Audio MIDI Setup → MIDI Studio → Bluetooth), or turn the Mac’s Bluetooth off.
  • iPhone, keeps failing: in the iPhone’s Settings → Bluetooth, tap the WIDI and Forget This Device, turn Bluetooth off and on again, then tap Connect in the app once more.
  • iPhone says Bluetooth isn’t allowed: tap Open Settings in the app. It opens Fractal Remote’s own page in the iPhone’s Settings: switch Bluetooth on there, then come back and tap Connect.
  • iPhone says Bluetooth is blocked by Screen Time (or by whoever manages the phone, such as a school or a company): in the iPhone’s Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions, allow Bluetooth Sharing, or ask whoever manages the phone. Fractal Remote’s own page in Settings can’t undo this one, which is why the app shows no Open Settings button for it.
  • Android finds nothing: allow Nearby devices for Fractal Remote (on Android 11 and older, allow Location and turn Location on), bring the phone close to the adapter, and tap Connect again.

It worked, then dropped

  • When the app has been closed, or left in the background for a while, the phone lets the Bluetooth connection go to save power. Open the app and tap Connect again.
  • Range: about 30 feet with nothing in the way. Walls, metal and a crowd shorten it. Keep the phone where it can “see” the adapter.
  • On a power bank: it may have switched itself off (see About power banks, above).

Still stuck

  • Unplug the Uhost’s power for a few seconds and plug it back in, then tap Stop and Connect in the app.
  • If the C2MIDI Pro was ever set up with CME’s own software, or acts strangely, hold its button for more than 5 seconds to put it back to how it came.
  • As a last resort, reset the Uhost: hold its button while you plug its power in, and let go after the green light flashes 3 times.
  • Then send a bug report from the app while it is still connected: Settings → Feedback → “Something is broken”. It carries what the app saw, which is what explains it. Say which unit and which adapter.

Other adapters

None of these has been tried with Fractal Remote, which is why there are no links to buy them here. Whatever you use, it has to plug into both of the unit’s MIDI jacks, In and Out. A MIDI jack carries messages one way only, so an adapter in one jack can send to the unit or hear it, never both, and the app needs the unit’s answers. Update any CME adapter with CME’s WIDI app first: some older firmware mangled the long messages the app sends.

  • CME WIDI Jack, with the TRS-35 cables, for the AM4: one small box with two leads, one for each 3.5 mm jack. Power it from USB-C. It has a Type A/B switch: on the AM4 start on A, and try B if nothing gets through.
  • CME WIDI Jack, with the 5-pin cables, for the FM3, FM9 and Axe-Fx III.
  • CME WIDI Master, for the FM3, FM9 and Axe-Fx III. It comes in two parts: the main plug goes in MIDI OUT and the small second plug on its short lead goes in MIDI IN. Both have to be in. It takes its power from the unit’s MIDI OUT, so it may not run on an AM4, and people report it running hot on an FM3.
  • Yamaha MD-BT01: two plugs, In and Out, and used by other people with Fractal units for years. Yamaha supports it on iPhone, not officially on Android, and it is hard to find now.
  • A WIDI Uhost plugged straight into the unit’s USB socket, with no MIDI cable: not tried. On an FM3 it can’t work at all, because the FM3’s USB socket doesn’t carry MIDI.
