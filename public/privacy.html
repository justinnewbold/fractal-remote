Privacy

Fractal Remote · last updated 25 October 2026

This app collects as little as it can. Your phone talks to your computer, your computer talks to your unit, and what passes between them is control messages — a preset, a scene, a knob — passed along and not written down. The demo, and the computer app on its own, need no account and send nothing about you anywhere — and a phone that opens the computer's own address on the same wifi sends nothing anywhere either: nothing leaves the room.

Everything below is about the parts that do use the internet. Each one is something you choose to turn on, except two: the phone app asking whether it is unlocked when it opens, described under Unlocking the full version, and the apps checking for updates, at the end of Where it is kept. Neither carries a name or an email address.

What is collected, and only if you ask for it

An account — only if you make one

The demo needs no account, and neither does the computer app on its own. Joining the two — so the phone can drive the unit plugged into your computer — is what an account is for: the two ends find each other by both being signed into the same account. The same account is what carries a full version you bought, and your setlists, to your other devices. Anybody can make one, before buying or without buying at all.

A phone on the same wifi as the computer can still reach it directly, with no account and nothing signed into, by opening the address the computer shows.

The commands between your phone and your computer pass through a relay while you are connected this way — a preset change, a scene, a knob. They are passed along, not written down. Over Bluetooth (beta) they go from the phone to the adapter on your unit, through no relay and no internet at all.

Unlocking the full version — a purchase record only if you buy it

The unlock is a one-time purchase. On a phone it is made through the App Store or Google Play, and in a browser through a checkout run by RevenueCat and Stripe. Your card details go to them and never to this app or its builder.

RevenueCat keeps the record of the purchase so the unlock follows your account to your other devices. It holds which account or device made the purchase, when, and through which store — not your name, card or address. Apple's, Google's and Stripe's own privacy policies cover the payment itself.

To know whether a phone is unlocked, the phone app asks RevenueCat each time it opens, bought or not, under a number: the account's if you are signed in, otherwise one made up for that phone. Never your name or email address. So RevenueCat also notes when the app last asked, which version of the app and of the phone's system it was, and which country it was in.

Help setting up the computer. The phone drives your unit through a free app on your computer. If no computer app has signed in with your account a day after the full version arrives on it, one email goes to your address with the download link and the three steps to set it up, and one reminder at least four days later if there is still none. Never more than those two, none at all once a computer has signed in, and none to an address that was never confirmed. A reply goes to Justin. To know when to send them, a few dates are kept with your account: when it was first seen with the full version, when each of those two emails went or was refused, and when Justin left you out of them, if he did. So is the first time a computer app signed in with it, which is kept for every account (see An account).

Your setlists — only if you are signed in

So the setlist you built on the couch is on your phone at the gig. It holds the names and order of the songs you saved and which presets you starred. Nothing else about your presets, and no audio of any kind.

Free access for a tester — only if Justin gives it to you

Justin, who builds the app, sometimes gives a tester the full version for nothing, using the email address the tester gave him. If that address already has an account, the full version is added to it straight away. If it has none yet, the address goes on a short waiting list — the address and the date it was added, nothing else — and the first time somebody signs in with it, the full version is added and the address comes off the list.

Either way, one email goes to that address to say so, headed "You have full access to Fractal Remote", with how to sign in and where to get the apps. It is sent when the access is given, and that is the only time, apart from the help setting up the computer described under Unlocking the full version.

The log is a record of what the app did — buttons pressed, requests made, errors. It is gathered the moment you press Send and at no other time, you can read the whole thing first, and you can send the report without it. A suggestion never carries one, and the database itself refuses to store one against a suggestion.

Send logs to developer, on the log page itself, sends a bug report at once: this session’s log, with a note if you type one in the box beside the button and nothing written if you don’t. When the app is connected to the Fractal Remote app on a computer, it also carries the computer’s own record of its link to your unit — whether its connection to the unit is open, the ports it can see, and the last lines it logged — the same record the website’s Copy Logs puts in a paste.

A log that goes with a report starts with the same few lines a copied log does: the app’s version, your unit (and, from the phone app, the preset), and which computer the app is connected to and its version, or, over Bluetooth (beta), the name of the Bluetooth adapter it is connected to instead (its name, never its Bluetooth address). From the phone app it also says which store version of the app is installed. On a phone it also carries the end of the last few times the app was open (the app keeps the last 120 lines of each of its last three runs on the phone, so the run that ended in a crash can be read), under its own heading, the same as Copy Logs does.

Screenshots. You can add up to three pictures to a report, on the log page, the website’s Debug log or the Feedback form. Only the ones you choose: on a phone you pick them in the phone’s own photo picker (Choose a screenshot), which shows your photos to you and hands the app only the ones you pick, or copy a screenshot and paste it in (the app reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste a screenshot, and your phone may ask you to allow that); on the website you pick a file. Each is made smaller before it is sent. They are stored privately for a week, readable by nobody through the app, not even by you, and they go to the person who builds the app with the report’s email. A screenshot shows whatever was on the screen, so add only ones you are happy for him to see.

The log holds no preset contents, no audio, no password, and nothing else you have typed into the app. A report sent while signed in is linked to that account, so that deleting the account deletes the report, and its screenshots, too. A copy of each report, with its log and screenshots, is also emailed to the person who builds the app when it is sent, and deleting a report from the database cannot reach that email: deleting the account tells him which reports went, and he deletes those emails too.

An answer. If you type an email address into the report, a reply goes there. If you leave it empty and are signed in, a reply goes to the email address of the account that sent it, the one you sign in with. Nothing else is done with it.

What is never collected

  • No analytics service and no tracking of any kind. There is no analytics code in this app, no advertising identifier, and no third-party tracking code. Nothing tells anybody which screens you open, what you press or how long you play: the debug log described above stays on your phone or computer unless you send it with a report. The one count there is — how many accounts were used lately, on the builder's own pages described below — is worked out from sign-in times that are kept anyway, and names nobody. And RevenueCat shows the builder the purchase records described above as sales totals and charts on its own dashboard.
  • No location. The app never reads where you are from the phone, and does not ask for Location, with one exception that is Android's rule rather than ours. Bluetooth (beta) lets the phone talk to your unit through a Bluetooth MIDI adapter, with no computer. It is still being tested, so for now it is switched on only for the developer's own account. There is one gap: the first time version 1.87.1 opens after it is installed or updated, before its first update has arrived, an account with the full version can still see a Bluetooth (beta) card under Settings → Phone & computer. It goes once the app has downloaded that update, usually from the next time it is opened. Version 1.87.11 and later do not show it at all. Nothing is asked for unless Connect is tapped on the Bluetooth page. On Android 11 and older, Android lets no app look for a Bluetooth device without the Location permission, so on those phones the app asks for it when you tap Connect on the Bluetooth page, and uses it only to find the adapter, never to read where the phone is. Newer Android phones ask for Nearby devices instead, and an iPhone asks only to use Bluetooth. The country RevenueCat notes, under Unlocking the full version, is worked out by RevenueCat from the internet connection, not from the phone's location.
  • No contacts, camera or microphone, and nothing read from your photos. None of them are used. The only pictures the app ever sees or sends are the screenshots you choose to add to a report, described above: the phone’s photo picker shows your photos to you, not to the app.
  • No audio. This app sends control messages to your unit. It does not record, stream or process sound.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone for advertising or any other purpose.

Where it is kept, and who can see it

Purchase records are kept by RevenueCat. Accounts, setlists and reports are stored with Supabase, on servers in the United States. Every row is behind rules that tie it to the account that made it. Bug reports, and the screenshots added to them, can be written by anybody and read back by nobody through the app — only by the person who builds it.

What the person who builds it can see. Justin has a few pages in the app, under Settings → Developer, that open for his own account and nobody else's — the server checks which account is asking, not only the app. On them he can see:

  • Everyone with an account — each account's email address, when it was made, whether the address was confirmed, when it last signed in, and whether it has the full version. And the waiting list above.
  • One account, looked up by its email address — the same, plus what it bought, when and through which store; which kinds of device it is signed in on (the phone app, the computer app, or the website, told apart by the name the app or browser gives when it signs in) and when each last checked in; and the app version and phone system RevenueCat last saw.
  • How many people — how many accounts were used today, in the last 7 days and in the last 30, on which kinds of device, and on which app versions. A count, naming nobody.
  • Sales — each purchase: when, through which store, for how much, and by which account; and anybody who has paid twice, or paid on an account whose email was never confirmed, so he can put it right. Once he has, he can mark it handled. Then the account, which of those two it was, when he marked it, the time of the newest purchase it was about, and how many purchases it counted (with any card sale beside it) are kept, so it is not shown again unless they pay again.
  • Messages — the bug reports and suggestions described above, with their screenshots, in his email.
  • Paid but no computer yet — each account with the full version that no computer app has signed in with: its email address and whether it is confirmed, how and when it got the full version (which store, a test purchase, or given by hand), and which of the emails about setting up the computer have gone to it or been refused. He can leave an account out of those emails.

All of it is read from what the account service and RevenueCat already keep in order to sign you in and to know whether you have paid, apart from the dates kept for the help setting up the computer, under An account and Unlocking the full version, and the marks on Sales described above. Nothing else new is recorded for these pages: no screens, no buttons, no time spent in the app. To help somebody who cannot sign in, he can also send their address a new confirmation or password-reset email, or mark the address as confirmed by hand.

Emails this app sends (a link to confirm your address or reset a password, the desktop download link you asked for, the one telling a tester they have been given full access, the email about setting up the computer and its one reminder, and bug reports, with their log and screenshots, on their way to the person who builds it) go through Resend. RevenueCat, Supabase, Resend, Expo (the phone app's update checks) and GitHub (the desktop app's update checks) each hold only what is described on this page, use it only to provide their part of the service, and protect it at least as this page describes.

The desktop apps check for their own updates from GitHub, and the phone app checks for updates from Expo. The phone's update check carries a random number for that install, made up the first time the app runs, so Expo can tell one install from another; never your name or email address. The desktop's check carries no number at all. On the phone, the photographs on the gear pages are fetched from this website as you look through them. Besides that number, the phone's check sends what any app update needs: which phone system it is, the app's version and the update it already has, and, after a launch that crashed, the error message the app stopped on, so a broken update can be found and taken back. Nothing in it says who you are. Once a day the phone app also reads a small file from this website that says which version is the newest in the App Store and on Google Play, so it can tell you when the copy on your phone is older. That request carries nothing about you or your account.

How long it is kept

  • Your account and setlists — until you delete the account, or ask for it to be deleted.
  • An address the desktop download link was sent to — kept only as a one-way scrambled fingerprint, to stop the link being sent to it over and over.
  • An address on the testers' waiting list — until somebody first signs in with it, or until the access is taken back or you ask for it to be removed.
  • The dates kept for the help setting up the computer, and when a computer app first signed in — until you delete the account.
  • A sale marked handled — the mark is kept until you delete the account.
  • Bug reports — until the problem is fixed and long enough afterward to be sure, then deleted, and the email of each with them.
  • Screenshots added to a report — the stored copy is deleted a week after it was sent, or an hour after if its report never arrived. Until the report itself is deleted, the only other copy is in that report’s email.

Deleting your data

In the app: Settings → your account → Delete account. It deletes your account, your setlists, bug reports sent from that account and their screenshots, and every other record kept under it, straight away and for good. Each of those bug reports was also emailed to the person who builds the app when it was sent, with its log and screenshots, and the app cannot reach his inbox: Delete account sends him a note listing them, and he deletes those emails too. A full version bought in the App Store or on Google Play stays with your Apple ID or Google account, and Restore a purchase brings it back on a new account. One bought on the website or in the computer app, or given to you, belongs to the account and is deleted with it.

If you have not bought the full version, the app opens on the purchase page instead of Settings. Delete account is at the bottom of that page, and does the same.

Or by email: email justinnewbold@gmail.com and say what you want removed — your account, your setlists, a report you sent (and the email of it he received, with its screenshots), an address on the testers' waiting list, or all of it. No form and no account needed to ask. It will be done and you will get a reply confirming it.

You can also delete your setlists from inside the app at any time without asking anyone.

Children

This app is for people with guitar hardware and is not directed at children under 13. No account is knowingly created for one. If you believe a child has signed up, email the address above and it will be removed.

Changes

If what this app collects ever changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. Anything that would collect materially more than the above would be announced in the app before it happened, not buried here.
