Privacy

Fractal Remote · last updated 10 October 2026

This app collects as little as it can. Your phone talks to your computer, your computer talks to your unit, and what passes between them is control messages — a preset, a scene, a knob — passed along and not written down. The demo, and the computer app on its own, need no account and send nothing about you anywhere — and a phone that opens the computer's own address on the same wifi sends nothing anywhere either: nothing leaves the room.

Everything below is about the parts that do use the internet. Each one is something you choose to turn on, except two: the phone app asking whether it is unlocked when it opens, described under Unlocking the full version, and the apps checking for updates, at the end of Where it is kept. Neither carries a name or an email address.

What is collected, and only if you ask for it

An account — only if you make one

The demo needs no account, and neither does the computer app on its own. Joining the two — so the phone can drive the unit plugged into your computer — is what an account is for: the two ends find each other by both being signed into the same account. The same account is what carries a full version you bought, and your setlists, to your other devices. Anybody can make one, before buying or without buying at all.

A phone on the same wifi as the computer can still reach it directly, with no account and nothing signed into, by opening the address the computer shows.

WhatWhy
Your email addressTo sign you in, and to reset a password
Your passwordStored as a hash. It is never readable, by us or anyone.

The commands between your phone and your computer pass through a relay while you are connected this way — a preset change, a scene, a knob. They are passed along, not written down. Over Bluetooth (beta) they go from the phone to the adapter on your unit, through no relay and no internet at all.

Unlocking the full version — a purchase record only if you buy it

The unlock is a one-time purchase. On a phone it is made through the App Store or Google Play, and in a browser through a checkout run by RevenueCat and Stripe. Your card details go to them and never to this app or its builder.

RevenueCat keeps the record of the purchase so the unlock follows your account to your other devices. It holds which account or device made the purchase, when, and through which store — not your name, card or address. Apple's, Google's and Stripe's own privacy policies cover the payment itself.

To know whether a phone is unlocked, the phone app asks RevenueCat each time it opens, bought or not, under a number: the account's if you are signed in, otherwise one made up for that phone. Never your name or email address. So RevenueCat also notes when the app last asked, which version of the app and of the phone's system it was, and which country it was in.

Your setlists — only if you are signed in

So the setlist you built on the couch is on your phone at the gig. It holds the names and order of the songs you saved and which presets you starred. Nothing else about your presets, and no audio of any kind.

Free access for a tester — only if Justin gives it to you

Justin, who builds the app, sometimes gives a tester the full version for nothing, using the email address the tester gave him. If that address already has an account, the full version is added to it straight away. If it has none yet, the address goes on a short waiting list — the address and the date it was added, nothing else — and the first time somebody signs in with it, the full version is added and the address comes off the list.

Either way, one email goes to that address to say so, headed "You have full access to Fractal Remote", with how to sign in and where to get the apps. It is sent when the access is given, and that is the only time.

Bug reports and suggestions — only when you press Send

There are two kinds and they carry different things, deliberately.

The log is a record of what the app did — buttons pressed, requests made, errors. It is gathered the moment you press Send and at no other time, you can read the whole thing first, and you can send the report without it. A suggestion never carries one, and the database itself refuses to store one against a suggestion.

The log holds no preset contents, no audio, no password, and nothing else you have typed into the app. A report sent while signed in is linked to that account, so that deleting the account deletes the report too.

What is never collected

  • No analytics and no tracking of any kind. There is no analytics service in this app, no advertising identifier, and no third-party tracking code. Nothing tells anybody which screens you open, what you press or how long you play: the debug log described above stays on your phone or computer unless you send it with a report. The one count there is — how many accounts were used lately, on the builder's own pages described below — is worked out from sign-in times that are kept anyway, and names nobody.
  • No location. The app never reads where you are from the phone, and does not ask for Location, with one exception that is Android's rule rather than ours. Bluetooth (beta) lets the phone talk to your unit through a Bluetooth MIDI adapter, with no computer; it is part of the full version, for everyone who has unlocked the app. On Android 11 and older, Android lets no app look for a Bluetooth device without the Location permission, so on those phones the app asks for it when you tap Connect on the Bluetooth page, and uses it only to find the adapter, never to read where the phone is. Newer Android phones ask for Nearby devices instead, and an iPhone asks only to use Bluetooth. The country RevenueCat notes, under Unlocking the full version, is worked out by RevenueCat from the internet connection, not from the phone's location.
  • No contacts, photos, camera or microphone. None of them are used.
  • No audio. This app sends control messages to your unit. It does not record, stream or process sound.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone for advertising or any other purpose.

Where it is kept, and who can see it

Purchase records are kept by RevenueCat. Accounts, setlists and reports are stored with Supabase, on servers in the United States. Every row is behind rules that tie it to the account that made it. Bug reports can be written by anybody and read back by nobody through the app — only by the person who builds it.

What the person who builds it can see. Justin has a few pages in the app, under Settings → Developer, that open for his own account and nobody else's — the server checks which account is asking, not only the app. On them he can see:

  • Everyone with an account — each account's email address, when it was made, whether the address was confirmed, when it last signed in, and whether it has the full version. And the waiting list above.
  • One account, looked up by its email address — the same, plus what it bought, when and through which store; which kinds of device it is signed in on (the phone app, the computer app, or the website, told apart by the name the app or browser gives when it signs in) and when each last checked in; and the app version and phone system RevenueCat last saw.
  • How many people — how many accounts were used today, in the last 7 days and in the last 30, on which kinds of device, and on which app versions. A count, naming nobody.
  • Sales — each purchase: when, through which store, for how much, and by which account.
  • Messages — the bug reports and suggestions described above.

All of it is read from what the account service and RevenueCat already keep in order to sign you in and to know whether you have paid. Nothing new is recorded for these pages: no screens, no buttons, no time spent in the app.

Emails this app sends (a link to confirm your address or reset a password, the desktop download link you asked for, the one telling a tester they have been given full access, and bug reports on their way to the person who builds it) go through Resend. RevenueCat, Supabase and Resend each hold only what is described on this page, use it only to provide their part of the service, and protect it at least as this page describes.

The desktop apps check for their own updates from GitHub, and the phone app checks for updates from Expo. On the phone, the photographs on the gear pages are fetched from this website as you look through them. All of these are ordinary download requests.

How long it is kept

  • Your account and setlists — until you delete the account, or ask for it to be deleted.
  • An address the desktop download link was sent to — kept only as a one-way scrambled fingerprint, to stop the link being sent to it over and over.
  • An address on the testers' waiting list — until somebody first signs in with it, or until the access is taken back or you ask for it to be removed.
  • Bug reports — until the problem is fixed and long enough afterwards to be sure, then deleted.

Deleting your data

In the app: Settings → your account → Delete account. It deletes your account, your setlists, bug reports sent from that account, and every other record kept under it, straight away and for good. A full version you bought stays with your Apple ID or Google account, and Restore a purchase brings it back on a new account.

Or by email: email justinnewbold@gmail.com and say what you want removed — your account, your setlists, a report you sent, an address on the testers' waiting list, or all of it. No form and no account needed to ask. It will be done and you will get a reply confirming it.

You can also delete your setlists from inside the app at any time without asking anyone.

Children

This app is for people with guitar hardware and is not directed at children under 13. No account is knowingly created for one. If you believe a child has signed up, email the address above and it will be removed.

Changes

If what this app collects ever changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top moves. Anything that would collect materially more than the above would be announced in the app before it happened, not buried here.
